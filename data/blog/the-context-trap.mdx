# From Small Fixes to Big Architecture: Lessons in Skill Context Optimisation

Two years ago, I started using AI IDEs like Cursor for small, contained problems. It was a neat trick: I’d paste a function, ask for a tweak, and get a result. Simple.

A year later, I was using it for full sprint tasks. It felt natural—like we were evolving together. AI models got smarter, and we all collectively got better at offloading larger, more complex architectural tasks to them. But that progress brought its own set of problems.

### The Context Trap

Even with the smartest models, I hit a wall recently. I realized I was forcing my agent to handle increasingly broad tasks without giving it a clear foundation. My solution? I tried to dump everything into its context—every rule, every pattern, every piece of documentation.

I thought, *if I just give it more context, it’ll be smarter.* 

It wasn't.

A few weeks ago, I noticed my agent started hallucinating. It was getting bogged down in outdated patterns and ignoring the core architecture I was trying to build. I kept pushing more data in, assuming the "more = better" rule applied. It wasn't being lazy; it was drowning in the noise I was creating.

### The Real Lesson: Optimise, Don't Dump

I finally realized that even the most capable models need structure. I had been ignoring my own rulebook: skills and rules need to be optimised, not just accumulated.

I stopped dumping every single file into the context window. Instead, I went back and refined my skill definitions—focusing only on the interface signatures, the core usage patterns, and the "why" behind the code. I tightened the context, culled the outdated rules, and focused on what was actually relevant to the current task.

The result? The hallucinations stopped, and the agent’s output became significantly sharper. 

### Why This Matters

Trust isn't just about the models getting better. It's about you becoming a better editor of the context you provide. 

If your AI agent is giving you shallow answers, don't blame the model immediately. Look at what you’re feeding it. Are you dumping 20 files into the chat? Are you giving it legacy code alongside new architecture? 

The goal is to help the model understand your intent without forcing it to sift through a haystack. When you get that balance right, it feels like you're working with a second brain, rather than a glorified copy-paste machine.

Moving from dumping context to *optimising* it was the quiet revolution in my workflow. Maybe it’s time you checked your context window, too.
