# The Context Trap: Why Less is Often More

Two years ago, AI IDEs were perfect for quick, local fixes. Today, we're offloading entire sprint tasks, and that shift has birthed a bad habit: the "context dump." We have this nagging feeling that if we shove every file in the repo into the chat window, the agent will somehow "get" our architecture.

It doesn’t. It just gets loud, hallucinates more, and slows down.

### Precision over volume

The worst offender is how we handle component libraries.

The sloppy way: Dumping the entire `/components` folder into the chat. Now, the model has to burn tokens parsing 50 unrelated files, messy legacy CSS, and redundant utilities. It loses the signal.

The surgical way:
*   Only feed it the interface definitions for the components you're actually calling.
*   Use `grep` or your IDE's search to extract specific `cva` or style definitions.

When you hand the model the exact interface and style constraints, you’re giving it the source of truth without the haystack. It’s cleaner, faster, and much more accurate.

### Delegate, don't dump

Stop trying to force one session to handle your whole codebase. When you’re building something complex—like a multi-step auth flow—don't try to cram the entire system into your main chat context.

Delegate. Fire up a separate sub-agent with its own isolated context. By offloading a scoped slice of the task, you keep your main session lean. Let the main agent handle the architecture while the sub-agent sweats the implementation details of that specific feature.

### Why this matters

Getting better results from AI isn't about using a bigger context window; it's about being a better editor. If the AI is giving you shallow, generic code, stop blaming the model. Look at what you're feeding it.

The goal isn't to dump a mountain of documentation; it's to pass the *right* signal. Once you start pruning your context, you stop wrestling with a glorified search-and-replace machine and start working with a partner that actually understands the "why" of your code.
