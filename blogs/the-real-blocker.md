# Why Understanding is the Real Blocker

In engineering, we love to talk about "blockers." Usually, we mean a missing API key, a broken CI/CD pipeline, or a tricky bug that’s been sitting in the backlog for a week. But after observing several sprints, I’ve realized the real culprit isn’t technical—it’s cognitive.

The most dangerous delays happen when everyone thinks they understand a user story, but no one actually does. 

We rush to build. We dive into the code, write the logic, and feel productive because we’re moving tickets across the board. But the silence—the moment of truth—usually comes right before production. That’s when the subtle misunderstandings start to bubble up. The edge cases weren’t defined, the requirements were interpreted differently by design and backend, and suddenly, the "done" feature is anything but.

The Lesson: Stop and breathe. If the team can’t explain the feature in plain English before the first commit, it’s not ready. Coding is the easy part; the hard part is ensuring we’re building the right thing. Don't let your speed be the enemy of your clarity.
